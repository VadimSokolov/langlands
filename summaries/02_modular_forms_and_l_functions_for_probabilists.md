How do modular forms and L functions look to a probabilist? The chapter develops, for probabilists, modular forms and cusp forms for SL(2,Z), Eisenstein series, Ramanujan's Delta function and tau function, Hecke operators and the Hecke relations (the three term recursion of the Chebyshev polynomials), Euler products, the Mellin transform and Hecke's functional equation, newforms of level N, Frobenius traces of elliptic curves and the Hasse bound, the modularity theorem, Dirichlet characters, and in outline automorphic representations of GL(n), standard and Rankin Selberg L functions and the generalized Ramanujan conjecture. It isolates two facts used throughout the book. By Deligne's theorem the normalised Hecke eigenvalues of a holomorphic newform are traces of elements of SU(2). The completed L function of Delta is the Mellin transform of the positive function Delta(iy), so its values on the central line, divided by the central value, form the characteristic function of a symmetric random variable, and the Riemann hypothesis for Delta says that this characteristic function has only real zeros. A heuristic of independent Sato Tate draws predicts that tau(p) vanishes for only finitely many primes, probably none (Lehmer's question). The chapter is expository and proves no new result.

## From the literature

Multiplicativity and the prime power recursion of tau (Mordell 1917) and the Ramanujan bound (Deligne 1971, 1974); Hecke operators and Euler products (Hecke 1937); the functional equation of Delta and the converse theorem at level one (Hecke 1936), extended to level N (Weil 1967); newforms (Atkin and Lehner 1970); the Hasse bound (Hasse 1933 to 1936); modularity of elliptic curves over the rationals (Wiles 1995; Taylor and Wiles 1995; Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor 2001); standard L functions of GL(n) (Godement and Jacquet 1972); Rankin Selberg L functions (Jacquet, Piatetski Shapiro and Shalika 1983; Shahidi 1981; Moeglin and Waldspurger 1989; Jacquet and Shalika 1981); the Ramanujan conjecture in weight one (Deligne and Serre 1974) and the bound 7/64 for Maass forms (Kim and Sarnak 2003); Lehmer's question (Lehmer 1947) and the density zero of the primes with tau(p) equal to zero (Serre 1981).
