# Chapter 16. Van Dantzig Pairs and Transport

How does the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) for automorphic L functions read in the languages of van Dantzig's problem (F and 1/F(it) both characteristic functions), Wald couples and generalized gamma convolutions (GGC)? For a self dual completion with nonnegative Pólya kernel (Riemann xi, Ramanujan Delta, eta products), GRH is equivalent to the reciprocal being the characteristic function of a symmetric Pólya frequency density, to a Wald couple of the Pólya variable with a GGC, and to Lukacs (Laguerre Pólya) class membership. The sine squared transport turns the Lévy measure of log f at a basepoint into the Thorin density of f(α)/f(α+√s), and, formally through Weil's explicit formula, the primes into the squared ordinates of the zeros; shifted basepoints give Thorin representations, unconditional from the edge of the critical strip, and a Thorin form of the Hinkkanen Lagarias criterion. Euler products give zeta subordinators with marginal and first passage (ballot) laws in generalized divisor functions, and a Hausdorff moment test on Taylor coefficients probes the Patie class of van Dantzig solutions. The equivalences are reformulations and prove no case of a Riemann hypothesis.

## From the literature

- The van Dantzig problem (van Dantzig 1958) and the Lukacs class (Lukacs 1968, 1970).
- Wald couples and the Wald couple form of the Riemann hypothesis (Roynette and Yor 2005), after Hartman's reciprocal Gamma theorem (Hartman 1976).
- The Lukacs class criteria for the Riemann hypothesis and the Lee Yang property, and the Patie class (Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar 2024), from Bernstein gamma functions (Patie and Savov 2018); Lee and Yang (1952), Newman (1974, 1976).
- Generalized gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992); examples of Ostrovskii (1970), Hinds (1974) and Biane, Pitman and Yor (2001).
- The positivity criterion for xi (Hinkkanen 1997; Lagarias 1999); Weil's explicit formula (Weil 1952); Kendall's identity (Kendall 1957); the ballot theorem (Takács 1967).

## New results

The Notes claim the equivalences and the shifted representation for automorphic L functions (for zeta, earlier work cited above), the damped cosine transport, the subordinator laws and the Patie test; the duality theorem restates Polson (2018) without its claim that the symmetric variable X is infinitely divisible, and the sine squared lemma is from that paper.

- [Duality of imaginary zeros, Thorin's condition, van Dantzig pairs and Wald couples](../RESULTS.md#thorin_condition_van_dantzig_wald_duality) (Theorem 16.5, page 259; proved): A real entire function of order at most one that is symmetric about a point has all its zeros on the vertical line through that point exactly when Thorin's condition holds (its reciprocal, centred there and evaluated along the square root, is the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution); when the centred function is also a moment generating function, this yields a van Dantzig pair and a Wald couple. Corrects a statement of Polson (2018, version 9 of 2026), Riemann, Thorin, van Dantzig pairs, Wald couples and Hadamard factorisation, arXiv:1804.10043.
- [Generalized Riemann hypothesis as a van Dantzig pair, a Wald couple and Lukacs class membership](../RESULTS.md#grh_van_dantzig_wald_lukacs_equivalence) (Theorem 16.6, page 260; proved): For an automorphic L function whose Pólya kernel is nonnegative, the generalized Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to three probabilistic statements (a Pólya frequency partner law, a Wald couple with a generalized gamma convolution, membership in the Lukacs class), as Roynette and Yor, Polson, and Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar showed for the Riemann zeta function; these are reformulations and prove no case of the hypothesis. New in this book.
- [Sine squared transport from a Lévy measure to a Thorin density](../RESULTS.md#sine_squared_transport_lemma) (Lemma 16.7, page 264; proved): When the logarithm of a positive analytic function has a Lévy Khintchine representation with a positive measure at a basepoint, its reciprocal along the square root is, up to a stable factor, a generalized gamma convolution transform whose Thorin density is the sine squared transport of that measure and can be read from the logarithmic derivative on one vertical line. First stated in Polson (2018, version 9 of 2026), Riemann, Thorin, van Dantzig pairs, Wald couples and Hadamard factorisation, arXiv:1804.10043.
- [Shifted Thorin representation of completed automorphic L functions and a Thorin form of the Hinkkanen Lagarias criterion](../RESULTS.md#shifted_thorin_representation_automorphic) (Theorem 16.8, page 266; proved): Moving the basepoint of a completed automorphic L function to the right of all its zeros makes its reciprocal along the square root a generalized gamma convolution transform with an explicit Thorin density, unconditionally from the edge of the critical strip on, and the generalized Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the positivity of these densities for every positive shift; this extends to automorphic L functions what Polson proved for the Riemann xi function. New in this book.
- [Damped cosine Lévy measure of the zeros and its sine squared transport](../RESULTS.md#damped_cosine_measure_sine_squared_transport) (Proposition 16.9, page 268; conditional on the generalized Riemann hypothesis for L(s, pi)): Assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis, the reciprocal of a completed automorphic L function with its basepoint moved a distance epsilon to the right of the centre, evaluated along the square root, factors into a positive one half stable transform and the exponential of a Thorin type integral whose density, a signed combination of Poisson kernels at the ordinates of the zeros, is the sine squared transport of the damped cosine measure of the zeros. New in this book.
- [Marginal law of the zeta subordinator through generalized divisor functions](../RESULTS.md#zeta_subordinator_marginal_law) (Proposition 16.10, page 273; proved): The compound Poisson subordinator built from the Euler product of the Riemann zeta function takes values in the logarithms of the positive integers, with probabilities given by the Dirichlet coefficients of a real power of zeta. New in this book.
- [First passage law of the zeta subordinator with drift, a ballot formula in generalized divisor functions](../RESULTS.md#zeta_subordinator_first_passage_law) (Theorem 16.11, page 274; proved): When a linear drift minus the zeta subordinator first passes above a level, the passage time is fixed by a random positive integer, the exponential of the subordinator at that time, whose law is an explicit ballot formula in generalized divisor functions, a Lagrange inversion for Dirichlet series. New in this book.
- [Hausdorff moment test for membership of completed L functions in the Patie class of van Dantzig solutions](../RESULTS.md#patie_class_hausdorff_test) (Proposition 16.13, page 276; proved): If a completed L function belonged to the class of van Dantzig solutions that Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar build from Bernstein gamma functions, the ratios of its central Taylor coefficients would be the values of the Laplace exponent at the integers and their second differences would form a completely monotone sequence, a necessary condition that can be tested numerically. New in this book.
