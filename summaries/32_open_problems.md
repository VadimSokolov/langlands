# Chapter 32. Open Problems

Where can probability say something definite about the open problems of the Langlands programme? This chapter states twelve problems, each with its status and attributions, what probability and computation contribute, and what would be needed; it proves no theorem and settles no case of any of them. Five restate known problems: the Ramanujan Petersson conjecture for Maass forms with Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture, read through Sarnak's density hypothesis as a large deviation bound; the Lang Trotter conjecture, read as a local limit theorem; Sato Tate groups of abelian varieties and motives with the Katz Sarnak symmetry types; functoriality of tensor products in general and of symmetric powers for Maass forms; and deterministic Ramanujan graphs of every degree. The book poses the other seven: Brownian bridge limits of the Sato Tate empirical process in families of elliptic curves, in a problem on discrepancy that starts from the conjecture of Akiyama and Tanigawa, an inverse problem asking whether twisted prime sums can detect closeness to a newform, a probabilistic proof of the Thorin form of the Riemann hypothesis for Ramanujan's Delta, the reach of the extreme value representation, second order Kemeny constants of supersingular isogeny graphs, Bayesian identification of Sato Tate groups of abelian surfaces, and the family law of the rank bias.

## From the literature

The discrepancy conjecture of Akiyama and Tanigawa (1999); Deligne's theorem for holomorphic forms (Deligne 1974); Selberg's bound 3/16 (Selberg 1965) and the bounds of Kim and Sarnak (2003); Plancherel equidistribution of Hecke eigenvalues (Sarnak 1987); infinitely many supersingular primes (Elkies 1987); Lang Trotter on average (Fouvry and Murty 1996; David and Pappalardi 1999); Sato Tate over totally real fields (Clozel, Harris, Shepherd Barron and Taylor 2008 and 2010; Barnet Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor 2011); the 52 Sato Tate groups of abelian surfaces (Fité, Kedlaya, Rotger and Sutherland 2012); symmetric power functoriality for holomorphic newforms (Newton and Thorne 2021); explicit near Ramanujan graphs (Mohanty, O'Donnell and Paredes 2022); Chebyshev's bias (Rubinstein and Sarnak 1994); generalized gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992).

## New results

The book's seven problems are catalogued as open.

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Brownian bridge limit of the Sato Tate empirical process in families of elliptic curves](../RESULTS.md#sato_tate_discrepancy_brownian_bridge_in_families) (Open Problem 32.3, page 584; open): Asks for which growth rates of the number of primes, measured against the conductor, the normalised Sato Tate empirical process of a random elliptic curve converges to a Brownian bridge and its scaled discrepancy to the Kolmogorov law. New in this book.
- [Inverse problem for automorphy from twisted sums over primes](../RESULTS.md#inverse_problem_for_automorphy_from_twisted_prime_sums) (Open Problem 32.4, page 585; open): Asks whether rates and fluctuations of the twisted prime sums of a sequence, at the square root scale and uniformly over Dirichlet characters, can decide whether the sequence is close to the normalised Hecke eigenvalues of a holomorphic newform. New in this book.
- [Hilbert's eighth problem for GL2 in Thorin form](../RESULTS.md#thorin_form_of_hilberts_eighth_problem_for_ramanujan_delta) (Open Problem 32.8, page 589; open): Asks for a probabilistic proof that the heat sum over the zeros of the L function of Ramanujan's Delta is completely monotone, a statement the book shows to be equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis for that L function. New in this book.
- [Extreme value representation for locating zeros and for other newforms](../RESULTS.md#extreme_value_representation_zeros_and_other_newforms) (Open Problem 32.9, page 590; open): Asks whether the law of the maximum in the extreme value representation of the L function of Ramanujan's Delta can locate or count its zeros, and which newforms admit a representation of the same kind. New in this book.
- [Second order term of Kemeny constants of supersingular 2 isogeny graphs](../RESULTS.md#second_order_kemeny_constants_of_supersingular_isogeny_graphs) (Open Problem 32.10, page 591; open): Asks whether the correction to the first order law for Kemeny constants of supersingular isogeny graphs has a limiting distribution across the characteristics, and whether it stays bounded. New in this book.
- [Bayesian identification of Sato Tate groups for abelian surfaces and effective bounds](../RESULTS.md#bayesian_identification_of_sato_tate_groups_for_abelian_surfaces) (Open Problem 32.11, page 592; open): Asks to extend the book's consistency theorem for Bayes factors between Sato Tate groups to the groups of abelian surfaces and to motives of higher rank, and to bound, polynomially in the logarithm of the conductor and without the Riemann hypothesis, how many primes the identification needs. New in this book.
- [Family limit law of the rank bias of elliptic curves](../RESULTS.md#family_limit_law_of_the_rank_bias_of_elliptic_curves) (Open Problem 32.12, page 592; open): Asks whether, assuming the Riemann hypothesis for the L function of each curve, the fluctuations of the rank bias in weighted sums of Frobenius traces, normalised by the logarithm of the conductor, have a limit law over the elliptic curves of a fixed analytic rank, zero or one, and whether it is the law predicted by their low lying zeros. New in this book.
