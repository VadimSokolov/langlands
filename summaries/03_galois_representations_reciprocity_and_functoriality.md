Which arithmetic objects lie behind the book's probabilistic statements, and which cases of the conjectures are proved? The Chebotarev density theorem is read as equidistribution of Frobenius classes for the uniform law on a finite Galois group. The chapter covers Artin L functions and the Artin conjecture, ℓ adic Galois representations and Serre's open image theorem, reciprocity for GL(1) (class field theory) and for GL(2) over the rationals (Galois representations of newforms and modularity), the Langlands dual group and the Satake isomorphism, functoriality and its known cases, the local Langlands correspondence, potential automorphy and the Sato Tate theorem, and Serre's Sato Tate group with the generalized Sato Tate conjecture. Computations for the S3 extension attached to the weight one form eta(z)eta(23z) of level 23 (the splitting field of x^3 minus x minus 1) at the primes below ten million show Chebotarev frequencies and a bias against completely split primes whose sign the prime squares explain. The chapter proves nothing new about reciprocity or functoriality.

## From the literature

The Chebotarev density theorem (Chebotarev 1926), effective under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (Lagarias and Odlyzko 1977); Artin L functions (Artin 1924); the Artin conjecture in the tetrahedral and octahedral cases (Langlands 1980; Tunnell 1981) and for odd two dimensional representations (Khare and Wintenberger 2009; Kisin 2009); Serre's open image theorem (Serre 1972); Galois representations of newforms (Eichler 1954; Shimura 1971; Deligne 1971; Deligne and Serre 1974; Ribet 1977); the Satake isomorphism (Satake 1963); functoriality (Langlands 1970) for symmetric powers (Gelbart and Jacquet 1978; Kim and Shahidi 2002; Kim 2003; Newton and Thorne 2021), base change (Langlands 1980; Arthur and Clozel 1989), quaternion algebras (Jacquet and Langlands 1970) and tensor products (Ramakrishnan 2000; Kim and Shahidi 2002); the local Langlands correspondence for GL(n) (Harris and Taylor 2001; Henniart 2000; Scholze 2013); the Sato Tate theorem through potential automorphy (Clozel, Harris and Taylor 2008; Taylor 2008; Harris, Shepherd Barron and Taylor 2010; Barnet Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor 2011); Sato Tate groups (Serre 2012) and their classification for abelian surfaces (Fité, Kedlaya, Rotger and Sutherland 2012); Chebyshev's bias (Rubinstein and Sarnak 1994).
