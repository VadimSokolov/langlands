# Chapter 15. Local Thorin Structure

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *On Langlands Conjectures: Probabilistic Methods* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 15. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *On Langlands Conjectures: Probabilistic Methods*, edition 1, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23419248. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/langlands/. How does the Thorin test of the previous chapter read for a single Euler factor? For a cuspidal representation of GL(2) over the rationals, the reciprocal of the completed Euler factor at an unramified prime, read in s = z^2, is, outside one degenerate case, the Laplace transform of a probability law exactly when the Ramanujan Petersson bound holds there, and is then a generalized gamma convolution (GGC) whose Thorin measure is a periodic comb: Ramanujan is local Thorin, as the generalized Riemann hypothesis is global Thorin. This equivalence proves no case of the Ramanujan conjecture. The local combs enter Weil's explicit formula damped by powers of the prime, and become point processes for Sato Tate random Euler products. Global Thorin is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields, hence unconditional by Weil's theorem, and to the Ramanujan property for regular graphs, which supersingular isogeny graphs have. By Keilson's theorem, passage times of the Hecke walk on the Bruhat Tits tree are GGCs whose atoms fill the Kesten band with the arcsine law, a central limit theorem and large deviations; hitting times on Ramanujan and isogeny graphs are exponential up to an error of order one over the number of vertices. The extreme value law of the Dedekind eta function is max infinitely divisible.

## From the literature

Ramanujan's conjecture (Ramanujan 1916), proved for holomorphic forms by Deligne (1974), and the bound 7/64 for Maass forms (Kim and Sarnak 2003); generalized gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992); the Pringsheim Landau theorem (Landau 1905; Widder 1941); Weil's explicit formula (Weil 1952; Iwaniec and Kowalski 2004); one level densities (Iwaniec, Luo and Sarnak 2000; Katz and Sarnak 1999); the Riemann hypothesis for curves (Weil 1948); Ramanujan graphs (Ihara 1966; Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak 1988; Pizer 1990); passage times of birth death processes (Keilson 1971; Kent 1980; Fill 2009); Kesten's spectral radius (Kesten 1959); the Aldous Brown inequality (Aldous and Brown 1992; Aldous and Fill 2002).

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Local Thorin criterion for a single Euler factor](../RESULTS.md#local_thorin_criterion_single_euler_factor) (Theorem 15.3, page 236; proved): The reciprocal of a completed Euler factor of degree two, read in the squared variable, is the Laplace transform of a probability law exactly when its trace parameter lies in the tempered interval with the degenerate endpoint 2 excluded, and the law is then a generalized gamma convolution whose Thorin measure is a periodic comb. New in this book.
- [Thorin criterion for the Ramanujan Petersson conjecture at a prime](../RESULTS.md#ramanujan_petersson_local_thorin_criterion) (Corollary 15.4, page 237; proved): For a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL(2) over the rationals with trivial central character, the Ramanujan Petersson conjecture at an unramified prime (outside the degenerate case of trace 2) holds exactly when the reciprocal of the symmetrised Euler factor, read in the squared variable, is the Laplace transform of a probability law, which is then a generalized gamma convolution whose Thorin measure is a periodic comb (Ramanujan is local Thorin, as the generalized Riemann hypothesis is global Thorin); the corollary is an equivalence and proves no case of the conjecture. New in this book.
- [Prime terms of the explicit formula as damped local Thorin combs](../RESULTS.md#explicit_formula_prime_terms_from_local_thorin_combs) (Proposition 15.8, page 239; proved): In Weil's explicit formula for the discriminant modular form, the contribution of each prime is the oscillatory part of the sum of the test function over that prime's local Thorin comb, with its sign changed and each Fourier coefficient damped by a power of the prime. New in this book.
- [Intensity of the Thorin point process of Sato Tate random Euler products](../RESULTS.md#sato_tate_thorin_point_process_intensity) (Proposition 15.10, page 241; proved): For Euler products with independent Sato Tate angles, the ordinates of the local Thorin combs form a point process with an explicit intensity that vanishes quadratically at the origin, and each local law has expected mean the square of the logarithm of the prime. New in this book.
- [Thorin criterion for the Riemann hypothesis of curves over finite fields](../RESULTS.md#thorin_criterion_riemann_hypothesis_curves_over_finite_fields) (Theorem 15.11, page 243; proved): For a curve over a finite field with q elements, none of whose Frobenius eigenvalues equals the square root of q, the reciprocal of its completed zeta function in the squared variable is the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution exactly when the Riemann hypothesis for the curve holds, so by Weil's theorem global Thorin holds unconditionally there, with a Thorin measure made of finitely many periodic combs. New in this book.
- [Thorin criterion for Ramanujan graphs and supersingular isogeny graphs](../RESULTS.md#thorin_criterion_ramanujan_graphs) (Theorem 15.13, page 243; proved): A finite connected regular graph of degree q plus one, without the eigenvalue twice the square root of q, is Ramanujan exactly when the reciprocal of its completed Ihara zeta function, read in the squared variable, is the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution, and both hold unconditionally for supersingular isogeny graphs. New in this book.
- [Thorin structure of passage times of the Hecke walk on the regular tree](../RESULTS.md#thorin_structure_hecke_walk_passage_times_tree) (Theorem 15.15, page 245; proved): The first time that continuous time simple random walk on a regular tree (the Hecke walk, on the Bruhat Tits tree when the degree is a prime plus one) reaches distance n from its start is a generalized gamma convolution whose Thorin atoms, given by the zeros of the sphere polynomials of the tree, lie in the Kesten band and follow the arcsine law as n grows, with an exact mean, a variance linear in n and a central limit theorem; Thorin support in the Kesten band is the random walk form of the Ramanujan bound, and this unconditional theorem about the tree proves no case of the Ramanujan conjecture for automorphic forms. New in this book.
- [Large deviations of passage times of the Hecke walk on the regular tree](../RESULTS.md#large_deviations_passage_times_regular_tree) (Theorem 15.16, page 246; proved): Passage times of the Hecke walk on a regular tree satisfy a large deviation principle with an explicit rate function, computed from the arcsine limit of their Thorin atoms. New in this book.
- [Exponential approximation of hitting times on Ramanujan graphs](../RESULTS.md#exponential_hitting_times_ramanujan_graphs) (Theorem 15.18, page 249; proved): On a Ramanujan graph that is not bipartite, all Dirichlet rates of a target but the slowest lie in the Kesten band, and the hitting time of any vertex from the uniform start is exponential up to an explicit error of order one over the number of vertices. New in this book.
- [Unconditional exponential law for hitting times on supersingular isogeny graphs](../RESULTS.md#exponential_hitting_times_supersingular_isogeny_graphs) (Corollary 15.19, page 249; proved): On supersingular isogeny graphs the hitting time of any curve by a continuous time random walk from a uniform start is unconditionally exponential up to an explicit error of order one over the number of vertices. New in this book.
- [Max infinite divisibility of the extreme value law of the Dedekind eta function](../RESULTS.md#max_infinite_divisibility_eta_extreme_value_law) (Proposition 15.20, page 251; proved): The maximum of scaled exponential variables whose distribution function is the product behind the Dedekind eta function is max infinitely divisible, with a completely monotone exponent function, an exponent measure built from divisor sums, a Poisson splitting and the same structure for every multiplicative eta product; the Mellin transform of the exponent, also stated, is classical. New in this book.
