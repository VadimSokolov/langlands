How far does the book's dictionary extend to the Maass cusp forms, the other half of the automorphic spectrum of SL(2, Z)? The modular surface is a probability space, Brownian motion on it is ergodic, and, following McKean, the Selberg trace formula is a heat trace identity for Brownian loops. The chapter proves that Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture for a congruence subgroup, open in general, is equivalent to complete monotonicity of its Maass cusp form heat trace with every eigenvalue lowered by 1/4. This is a reformulation, not a proof: unconditionally it gives complete monotonicity for every congruence subgroup only after the smaller shift the Kim Sarnak bound allows, and the full statement for SL(2, Z), where the conjecture is known. Weyl's law keeps the spectrum from being a Thorin measure. Treating closed geodesics as Beurling primes, it proves an unconditional Thorin representation of the geodesic Euler product Z(s+1)/Z(s) at every basepoint to the right of 1, and a Poisson Dirichlet limit for random geodesic integers. It also reads the Ramanujan conjecture for Maass forms at a prime as local Thorin positivity, places even icosahedral Artin representations among Maass forms of eigenvalue 1/4, and states the Eichler Selberg trace formula behind vertical Sato Tate.

## From the literature

- Maass forms (Maass 1949), the trace formula, the Selberg zeta function and Weyl's law (Selberg 1956; Hejhal 1976, 1983), and the eigenvalues of SL(2, Z) (Hejhal 1992; Booker, Strömbergsson and Venkatesh 2006).
- The Kim Sarnak bound toward the Ramanujan and Selberg conjectures (Kim and Sarnak 2003).
- The hyperbolic heat kernel and the Brownian reading of the trace formula (McKean 1972).
- The prime geodesic theorem (Huber and Selberg; Sarnak 1982 for SL(2, Z); Soundararajan and Young 2013) and reciprocal geodesics (Sarnak 2007).
- Automorphy of dihedral (Maass 1949), tetrahedral (Langlands 1980) and octahedral (Tunnell 1981) Artin representations.
- The Eichler Selberg trace formula (Eichler 1957, Selberg 1956; Zagier 1977) and vertical Sato Tate (Serre 1997; Conrey, Duke and Farmer 1997).

## New results

None of the catalogued theorems proves a new case of the Selberg or Ramanujan conjectures.

- [Ramanujan conjecture at a prime as local Thorin positivity for Maass forms](../RESULTS.md#ramanujan_at_a_prime_as_local_thorin_positivity_for_maass_forms) (Corollary 26.1, page 485; proved): A normalised Euler factor of a Hecke Maass form at a prime, read in the square root of the Laplace variable, is the Laplace transform of a probability law, and then of a generalized gamma convolution with an explicit Thorin comb, exactly when the Hecke eigenvalue obeys the Ramanujan bound with the value 2 excluded, which restates the Ramanujan conjecture at that prime without proving it. New in this book.
- [Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture is equivalent to complete monotonicity of the Maass cusp form heat trace](../RESULTS.md#selberg_eigenvalue_conjecture_as_complete_monotonicity) (Theorem 26.4, page 488; proved): Proves an equivalence and not the conjecture, which is open in general, namely that Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture for a congruence subgroup holds if and only if the heat trace of its Maass cusp forms, with every eigenvalue lowered by one quarter, is completely monotone; unconditionally it proves complete monotonicity only with the eigenvalues lowered by the smaller amount that the Kim Sarnak bound allows, for every congruence subgroup (part ii), and the full statement for SL(2, Z), where the conjecture is known (part iii), while Weyl's law keeps the spectrum from being a Thorin measure. New in this book.
- [Thorin representation of the geodesic Euler product Z(s+1)/Z(s)](../RESULTS.md#thorin_representation_of_the_geodesic_euler_product) (Theorem 26.5, page 491; proved): At every basepoint to the right of 1, the ratio of values of the geodesic Euler product Z(s+1)/Z(s) of a compact hyperbolic surface or of the modular surface, read in the square root of the Laplace variable, is unconditionally the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution with an explicit nonnegative Thorin density divided by that of a one half stable law, and the same holds for Dedekind zeta functions and for the Rankin Selberg L function of an automorphic representation with its contragredient. New in this book.
- [Poisson Dirichlet limit for random geodesic integers](../RESULTS.md#random_geodesic_integers_are_poisson_dirichlet) (Theorem 26.6, page 493; proved): A random product of closed geodesics drawn with zeta type weights near the pole behaves like a random integer, with Gamma process and Poisson Dirichlet limits, Dickman's law for the largest factor and a normal law for the number of distinct factors. New in this book.
- [Thorin representation of the geodesic Euler product at the centre](../RESULTS.md#thorin_representation_of_the_geodesic_euler_product_at_the_centre) (Open Problem 26.1, page 498; open): Asks whether, closer to the centre of the critical strip, the geodesic Euler product of a compact surface still has a positive Thorin density once its pole is removed, despite divergent spectral sums. New in this book.
- [A Chebotarev theorem for the closed geodesics of the modular surface](../RESULTS.md#chebotarev_theorem_for_closed_geodesics) (Open Problem 26.2, page 498; open): Asks for the densities of arithmetic labels of the closed geodesics of the modular surface ordered by length, and for the matching Dirichlet process limit and Mertens constants. New in this book.
- [Even icosahedral Artin representations and Maass forms of eigenvalue one quarter](../RESULTS.md#even_icosahedral_representations_and_maass_forms_of_eigenvalue_one_quarter) (Open Problem 26.3, page 498; open): Proposes testing numerically the complete monotonicity of the Galois heat trace of an even icosahedral Artin representation and searching for the Maass form of eigenvalue one quarter that Artin's conjecture predicts. New in this book.
- [Cutoff for Brownian motion on the principal congruence covers X(N)](../RESULTS.md#cutoff_for_brownian_motion_on_congruence_covers) (Open Problem 26.4, page 498; open): Asks whether Brownian motion on the principal congruence covers of the modular surface mixes abruptly, with cutoff at a time of order log N as for random walks on expanders, and how the cusps affect the profile. New in this book.
