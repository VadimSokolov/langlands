# Chapter 8. Random Groups: Class Groups, Selmer Groups and Ranks

Do class groups, Selmer groups and Shafarevich Tate groups behave like random groups, and what do such models predict for ranks of elliptic curves? The chapter builds the Cohen Lenstra measure, which weights a finite abelian p group by one over its number of automorphisms, from cokernels of Haar random p adic matrices, proving in this one model Hall's identity, the Friedman Washington limit, the p rank law and the moments. It presents the Cohen Lenstra conjecture, the Davenport Heilbronn theorem on 3 torsion in quadratic class groups with its secondary term, the Poonen Rains model of p Selmer groups as intersections of random maximal isotropic subspaces, written as a sum of independent Bernoulli coins, the Bhargava Shankar averages, the BKLPR and Park Poonen Voight Wood models, Goldfeld's conjecture, the minimalist conjecture and parity, and the average rank bounds that Selmer data give. PARI/GP data on quadratic class groups and on 2 Selmer groups and ranks of 6121 curves of small height test the models; the secondary term explains the observed 3 torsion deficit. The chapter marks no result as its own.

## From the literature

- Hall's identity (Hall 1938) and the random p adic matrix model (Friedman and Washington 1989).
- The Cohen Lenstra heuristics (Cohen and Lenstra 1984), extended by Cohen and Martinet (1990) and corrected at roots of unity by Malle (2008).
- Mean 3 torsion of quadratic class groups (Davenport and Heilbronn 1971) and its secondary term (Bhargava, Shankar and Tsimerman 2013; Taniguchi and Thorne 2013).
- Cohen Lenstra over function fields as q grows, and uniqueness from moments (Ellenberg, Venkatesh and Westerland 2016; Wood 2017).
- The Poonen Rains model (Poonen and Rains 2012), the BKLPR model (Bhargava, Kane, Lenstra, Poonen and Rains 2015), Delaunay's heuristic (Delaunay 2001) and the bounded rank heuristic (Park, Poonen, Voight and Wood 2019).
- Average sizes 3, 4 and 6 of 2, 3 and 5 Selmer groups (Bhargava and Shankar 2015; the last a 2013 preprint).
- Goldfeld's conjecture (Goldfeld 1979), 2 parity (Monsky 1996) and p parity (Dokchitser and Dokchitser 2010).
