# Chapter 6. Murmurations: Root Numbers in Frobenius Data

Can the root number of an elliptic curve be read off from its Frobenius traces? For a single curve the traces a_p are Sato Tate distributed and forget the sign of the functional equation, but averaged over a family of curves with conductor in a window, at the scale p/N, the traces separate the two root numbers and oscillate with a period proportional to the conductor. These oscillations are called murmurations. The chapter defines the murmuration average, explains through the Eichler Selberg trace formula why the natural variable is y = p/N, and reports a computation on all 30,366 isogeny classes of conductor between 2500 and 10000 in Cremona's tables, with the ranks separated. It then treats the recovery of root numbers as Bayesian classification: a murmuration model with a score, a proposition giving the error of the sign classifier (a Hoeffding bound and an exact Gaussian Bayes error), a lemma on the size of the signal over primes, and a calibration against held out data. It places murmurations against the one level density at the edge of the support and against the Thorin measures, and recomputes them in Ramanujan's family of level one forms in the weight aspect. Three open problems are posed. The chapter proves no case of a conjecture of the Langlands programme.

## From the literature

Murmurations were found by He, Lee, Oliver and Pozdnyakov (2022), explained by Sarnak through the Eichler Selberg trace formula (2023) and proved for weight two newforms by Zubrilina (2023). The weight aspect (Bober, Booker, Lee and Lowry Duda), Dirichlet characters (Lee, Oliver and Pozdnyakov) and height ordered curves (Sawin and Sutherland 2025) followed. Related tools are Zagier (1977) for the level one trace formula, Iwaniec, Luo and Sarnak (2000) for the one level density by root number, and Ramanujan (1916), Mordell (1917) and Deligne (1974) for the tau function.

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Error of the murmuration score classifier for root numbers](../RESULTS.md#murmuration_model_root_number_classifier) (Proposition 6.6, page 82; proved): In a model where the normalised Frobenius traces of a curve are independent given the root number with means equal to the root number times a weight, the sign of the weighted trace sum recovers the root number with error at most the exponential of minus one eighth of the signal, and in the Gaussian case it is the Bayes rule with error exactly the normal tail at the square root of the signal. New in this book.
- [Asymptotic of the murmuration signal over primes in a dyadic range](../RESULTS.md#prime_sum_asymptotic_for_murmuration_signal) (Lemma 6.7, page 83; proved): For a continuous function of bounded variation, the sum of its square at p over N divided by p, over primes between y0 N and y N, is asymptotic to the integral of the square over t divided by the logarithm of N, so the signal of the murmuration model over such primes is of order one over the logarithm of the conductor. New in this book.
- [Convergence of the murmuration density for elliptic curves ordered by conductor](../RESULTS.md#conductor_ordering_murmuration_density_open_problem) (Open Problem 6.1, page 90; open): Prove that the murmuration density of isogeny classes of elliptic curves over the rationals ordered by conductor converges and identify its limit, in particular whether it equals the density for all weight two newforms or is changed by the restriction to rational coefficients. New in this book.
- [Rate of the Bayes error for root numbers in the murmuration model with rank bias](../RESULTS.md#posterior_concentration_murmuration_open_problem) (Open Problem 6.2, page 90; open): In the murmuration model with the rank bias added, find the rate at which the Bayes error for the root number tends to zero or to a positive limit as the cutoff and the conductor grow, decide whether the integral of the squared density over t governs it, and explain why the observed scores are more concentrated than the model predicts. New in this book.
- [Murmurations at the edge of the support of the one level density in Thorin form](../RESULTS.md#thorin_edge_of_murmurations_open_problem) (Open Problem 6.3, page 90; open): Show, assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis for the family, that the Fourier transform of the root number weighted average of rescaled Thorin measures, after subtracting its random matrix limit, converges in a window of width of order one over the logarithm of X around the edge, with the limit determined by the murmuration density; the authors doubt this is the right formulation. New in this book.
