# Chapter 17. Squarefree Integers and Thorin Atoms

From *On Langlands Conjectures: Probabilistic Methods* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 17. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *On Langlands Conjectures: Probabilistic Methods*, edition 1, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23503453. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/langlands/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/langlands.

Squarefree integers have Dirichlet series ζ(s)/ζ(2s), whose poles at ρ/2 come from the nontrivial zeros ρ of the Riemann zeta function. The chapter reads this through the Pólya density of the Riemann xi function and the Thorin measure of its reciprocal: under the Riemann hypothesis (RH) the poles in the strip 0 < Re s < 1/2 correspond to the Thorin atoms, and the constant in the sum of μ(n)²/n is a combination of two tilted Pólya means, with a number field analogue. RH is equivalent to an x^{1/4+ε} error for the exponentially smoothed squarefree count, and to a mean square bound for the squarefree error. After Gamma(2) smoothing the squarefree error is exactly minus half the mean of the Mertens function at a random point, and Gamma(ν) smoothing gives a positive average of it exactly when ν ≤ 2. Off the critical line each pair of zeros contributes an exponential time plus a Brownian passage time, smearing its atom into a nearly Cauchy profile. For imaginary quadratic fields a Pólya kernel is positive when |d| < π² and takes negative values whenever 2 and 3 are inert. The RH equivalences are reformulations and prove no case of RH.

## From the literature

- Sums of μ(n)²/n^α (Mathematics Stack Exchange 2016).
- Ω(x^{1/4}) for the squarefree error (Evelyn and Linfoot 1931; Stark 1966); oscillations beyond 3x^{1/4} and Keith Conrad's descent for the sharp count (Mossinghoff, Oliveira e Silva and Trudgian 2021); on RH, x^{1/3+ε} (Montgomery and Vaughan 1981) and x^{11/35+ε} (Liu 2016); short interval variance (Gorodetsky, Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Rodgers 2021).
- Pólya's kernel (Riemann 1859; Pólya 1926) and its positivity, credited by Biane, Pitman and Yor (2001) to Chung and to Newman (1976).
- The positivity criterion for xi (Hinkkanen 1997; Lagarias 1999); the shifted Thorin family of xi (Polson 2018).
- Lazarević's inequality (Lazarević 1966); no positive real zero of L(s, χ_d) for -3·10^8 ≤ d < 0 (Watkins 2004).

## New results

The Notes call the Gamma(2) Mertens representation, the threshold ν ≤ 2 and the two kernel sign theorems new as far as the authors know; the zeta Thorin equivalence is from Polson (2017).

- [Squarefree constant as two tilted Pólya means and a sum over the zeros of zeta](../RESULTS.md#squarefree_constant_tilted_polya_means) (Theorem 17.4, page 285; proved): The constant in the asymptotic formula for the sum of the reciprocals of the squarefree integers up to x is an explicit elementary constant plus a combination of two tilted means of the Pólya law of the Riemann xi function, equivalently a sum over the zeros of zeta. New in this book.
- [Riemann hypothesis as the generalized gamma convolution property of the reciprocal xi function](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_xi_ggc_riemann_hypothesis) (Proposition 17.5, page 285; proved): The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the reciprocal of the Riemann xi function, centred at one half and evaluated along the square root, being the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution, whose Thorin atoms are then the squared ordinates of the zeros; this is a reformulation, not a proof. First stated in Polson (2017, version 26 of 2026), On Hilbert's 8th problem, arXiv:1708.02653.
- [Riemann hypothesis for a Dedekind zeta function as a generalized gamma convolution property](../RESULTS.md#dedekind_zeta_thorin_ggc_equivalence) (Proposition 17.6, page 286; proved): For a number field whose Dedekind zeta function does not vanish at the centre, the Riemann hypothesis for that function is equivalent to the reciprocal of its completion along the square root being a generalized gamma convolution transform with Thorin atoms at the squared ordinates of the zeros; this is a reformulation, not a proof. New in this book.
- [Riemann hypothesis and the exponentially smoothed squarefree count](../RESULTS.md#exponentially_smoothed_squarefree_count_rh) (Theorem 17.7, page 287; proved): The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to an error of order x to the power one quarter plus epsilon in the exponentially smoothed count of squarefree integers, and under the Riemann hypothesis with simple zeros the smoothed count has an explicit expansion over the halved zeros. New in this book.
- [Riemann hypothesis and the mean square of the squarefree error](../RESULTS.md#squarefree_error_mean_square_rh) (Theorem 17.8, page 288; proved): The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to a bound of order X to the power three halves plus epsilon for the integral, over the dyadic interval from X to 2X, of the square of the error term in the count of squarefree integers. New in this book.
- [Gamma smoothed squarefree count under the Riemann hypothesis, uniformly in the shape](../RESULTS.md#gamma_smoothed_squarefree_count_bound_uniform_in_shape) (Proposition 17.10, page 289; conditional on the Riemann hypothesis): Assuming the Riemann hypothesis, averaging the squarefree count over a Gamma law of shape n at least one and mean x gives an error of order x to the one quarter times n to the one eighth, up to epsilon powers, uniformly in n. New in this book.
- [Gamma(2) smoothed squarefree error as an average of the Mertens function](../RESULTS.md#gamma2_smoothed_squarefree_error_mertens_representation) (Theorem 17.11, page 290; proved): After smoothing by a Gamma(2) law the error in the count of squarefree integers is exactly minus one half of the mean of the Mertens function at a random point with an explicit density, and that density is twice the gap between a continuous and an integer rate mixture of Gamma(2) densities. New in this book.
- [Gamma(2) smoothed squarefree count, the Riemann hypothesis and its explicit expansion](../RESULTS.md#gamma2_smoothed_squarefree_count_riemann_hypothesis) (Corollary 17.12, page 291; proved): The Gamma(2) smoothed count of squarefree integers has an error smaller than the square root of X unconditionally, the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to an error of order X to the one quarter plus epsilon, and under the Riemann hypothesis with simple zeros the smoothed count has an explicit expansion over the halved zeros. New in this book.
- [Sharp threshold for Gamma smoothing of the squarefree count as a positive average of the Mertens function](../RESULTS.md#gamma_smoothing_mertens_positivity_threshold) (Theorem 17.14, page 292; proved): Smoothing the count of squarefree integers by a Gamma law of shape nu turns its error into an average of the Mertens function with positive weights exactly when nu is at most 2, so Gamma(2) is the largest shape for which this holds. New in this book.
- [Constant of the logarithmic squarefree ideal sum over a number field as a sum over Dedekind zeros](../RESULTS.md#squarefree_ideal_constant_number_fields) (Theorem 17.16, page 294; proved): Over any number field, the constant in the asymptotic formula for the sum of the reciprocal norms of the squarefree ideals is an explicit term in the discriminant and the signature plus a sum over the zeros of the Dedekind zeta function. New in this book.
- [Brownian subordination of the reciprocal xi function off the critical line](../RESULTS.md#xi_off_critical_line_brownian_subordination) (Corollary 17.15, page 294; proved): With its basepoint at or to the right of the supremum of the real parts of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function in the critical strip, the reciprocal xi function along the square root is, unconditionally, the Laplace transform of a sum over pairs of conjugate zeros of an exponential time plus a Brownian first passage time, so each Thorin atom is smeared by its own Brownian clock. New in this book.
- [Pólya kernel of an imaginary quadratic Dedekind zeta function and its positivity for small discriminants](../RESULTS.md#imaginary_quadratic_polya_kernel_positivity) (Theorem 17.17, page 295; proved): The completed Dedekind zeta function of an imaginary quadratic field is the Fourier transform of an explicit even, doubly exponentially decaying kernel built from ideal counts, and this kernel is positive for the four fields whose discriminant is smaller than pi squared in absolute value. New in this book.
- [Pólya kernels of imaginary quadratic fields in which 2 and 3 are inert take negative values](../RESULTS.md#imaginary_quadratic_polya_kernel_sign_change) (Theorem 17.18, page 296; proved): For every imaginary quadratic field in which both 2 and 3 are inert, a set of infinitely many fields that includes those with discriminants -19, -43, -67 and -163, the Pólya kernel of the Dedekind zeta function takes negative values. New in this book.
- [How often the Pólya kernel of an imaginary quadratic field is positive](../RESULTS.md#density_of_positive_imaginary_quadratic_polya_kernels) (Open Problem 17.2, page 298; open): The book asks whether a positive proportion of imaginary quadratic fields have a positive Pólya kernel, and whether finitely many splitting conditions on small primes force positivity. New in this book.
