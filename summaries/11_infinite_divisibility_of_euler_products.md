# Chapter 11. Infinite Divisibility of Euler Products

When is an Euler product a characteristic function? Khinchine observed that for σ > 1 the ratio ζ(σ+it)/ζ(σ) is the characteristic function of a compound Poisson, hence infinitely divisible, law. For an automorphic L function the same expansion gives a signed Lévy Khintchine exponent. The chapter proves that for the discriminant form Δ, whose eigenvalue τ(2) is negative, the normalised L function is not a characteristic function for any σ > 1, and that the Rankin Selberg pole forces infinitely many negative Hecke eigenvalues for every holomorphic newform with real eigenvalues. Rankin Selberg convolutions restore positivity and, with Landau's lemma and isobaric sums, give L(1+it, π) ≠ 0 for unitary cuspidal π on GL(n), n ≥ 2, unless π twisted by |det|^(it) is self dual. Left of Re s = 1 the chapter reviews Bohr Jessen value distribution, universality, large deviations and random multiplicative functions. It then proves that the Sato Tate random Euler product, with independent Satake angles from the Sato Tate law, is a mean one martingale with second moment ζ(2σ) whose logarithm at a real point obeys a central limit theorem, with drift minus half the variance, as σ decreases to 1/2.

## From the literature

- The Lévy Khintchine formula, as in Sato (1999); infinite divisibility of the zeta distribution (Khinchine 1938).
- Landau's lemma (Landau 1905); nonvanishing of ζ on Re s = 1 (Hadamard 1896; de la Vallée Poussin 1896).
- Rankin Selberg L functions (Jacquet and Shalika 1981; Jacquet, Piatetski Shapiro and Shalika 1983; Shahidi 1981; Moeglin and Waldspurger 1989; Rudnick and Sarnak 1996); nonvanishing on Re s = 1 for GL(n) (Jacquet and Shalika 1976) and its positivity proof (Sarnak 2004).
- Value distribution and universality (Bohr and Jessen 1930, 1932; Jessen and Wintner 1935; Voronin 1975; Bagchi 1981; Laurinčikas and Matsumoto 2001), Selberg's central limit theorem (Selberg 1946), large deviations (Lamzouri 2011).
- Random multiplicative functions (Wintner 1944; Harper 2020); the Sato Tate theorem for Δ (Barnet Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor 2011).

## New results

The authors call the two results on the random Euler product elementary but have not found them stated in this form.

<!-- results:begin -->
- [The normalised L function of the discriminant form is not a characteristic function](../RESULTS.md#discriminant_form_l_function_not_characteristic_function) (Proposition 11.3, page 160; proved): Because the Ramanujan tau function takes negative values, on every vertical line to the right of the critical strip the L function of the discriminant modular form somewhere exceeds in modulus its value at the real point of that line, so, unlike the Riemann zeta function, it gives no characteristic function there. New in this book.
- [Mean and second moment of the Sato Tate random Euler product](../RESULTS.md#sato_tate_random_euler_product_mean_and_second_moment) (Proposition 11.12, page 167; proved): A random model of the L function of a cusp form, with independent Satake angles drawn from the Sato Tate law, converges as a martingale to the right of the critical line, with mean one and mean square equal to the Riemann zeta function at twice the real part. New in this book.
- [Central limit theorem for the Sato Tate random Euler product near the critical line](../RESULTS.md#sato_tate_random_euler_product_central_limit_theorem) (Theorem 11.13, page 167; proved): In the Sato Tate random model of the L function of a cusp form, the logarithm at a real point approaching the critical line is asymptotically normal, with a variance that grows logarithmically and a negative drift equal to half the variance. New in this book.
