# Chapter 12. Log Correlated Fields

How does the logarithm of an L function behave along a window of the critical line, rather than at one random point? The chapter treats log |ζ(1/2+it)| and log |L(6+it, Δ)|, for the Ramanujan discriminant form Δ, as log correlated fields, and presents Selberg's central limit theorem, the Keating Snaith model and the hybrid Euler Hadamard product, branching random walks and the Fyodorov Hiary Keating conjecture on maxima over unit windows, Gaussian multiplicative chaos, random multiplicative functions and pair correlation of zeros. Its own results are an elementary lemma explaining the exponential left tail at finite height by simple zeros, the covariance of the random Euler product field (a standard computation), first moment asymptotics, with and without a ballot constraint, for Brownian motion on a tree, a conjectured variance formula for log |L(6+it, Δ)| at finite height with an explicit arithmetic constant, and three translations into the Pólya variable, Thorin measure and extreme value language of later chapters, two assuming the Riemann hypothesis for Δ. Computations of L(s, Δ) at heights 500 to 2500 test the predictions; the translations are exact consequences of cited theorems, not evidence for or against the Riemann hypothesis.

## From the literature

- Selberg's central limit theorem (Selberg 1946; Tsang 1984; short proof by Radziwiłł and Soundararajan 2017), and conditional versions for other L functions (Selberg 1992; Bombieri and Hejhal 1995).
- Random matrix moments (Keating and Snaith 2000), the moment conjecture (Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein and Snaith 2005), the hybrid Euler Hadamard product (Gonek, Hughes and Keating 2007).
- Maxima of branching random walks (Bramson 1978; Addario Berry and Reed 2009; Aïdékon 2013).
- The Fyodorov Hiary Keating conjecture (Fyodorov, Hiary and Keating 2012), proved to first order (Najnudel 2018 under the Riemann hypothesis; Arguin, Belius, Bourgade, Radziwiłł and Soundararajan 2019) and to tightness (Arguin, Bourgade and Radziwiłł, preprints 2020 and 2023).
- Gaussian multiplicative chaos (Kahane 1985) and zeta as complex chaos (Saksman and Webb 2020).
- Better than square root cancellation for random multiplicative functions (Harper 2020).
- The n level correlations of automorphic L function zeros (Rudnick and Sarnak 1996).

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Exponential left tail of the log modulus near simple zeros](../RESULTS.md#exponential_left_tail_near_simple_zeros) (Proposition 12.4, page 175; proved): An elementary fact about real analytic functions with simple zeros, which the book applies to the Hardy function of the discriminant form to explain the exponential left tail of the empirical distribution of the log modulus of its L function on windows of the critical line whose zeros are simple. New in this book.
- [Variance at finite height of the log modulus of the L function of Delta](../RESULTS.md#finite_height_variance_conjecture_for_delta) (Conjecture 12.6, page 178; open): Conjectures an explicit formula for the variance of the log modulus of the L function of the discriminant form on its critical line at finite height, combining the random matrix constant with an arithmetic constant, obtained by writing out the moment conjecture and hybrid Euler Hadamard product heuristics for this form; the book's computations at heights 500 to 2500 agree with it within sampling error. New in this book.
- [Log correlated covariance of the random Euler product field](../RESULTS.md#covariance_of_random_euler_product_field) (Proposition 12.7, page 180; proved): Writes out the standard computation showing that the prime sum of the random Euler product, for the Riemann zeta function and for the discriminant form, is a log correlated field, whose covariance at distance u between 0 and 1 is one half of the logarithm of the smaller of 1/u and the logarithm of the prime cutoff, up to a bounded error. New in this book.
- [First moments with and without the ballot constraint for Brownian motion on a tree](../RESULTS.md#tree_brownian_motion_first_moments_with_ballot_constraint) (Proposition 12.8, page 181; proved): Asymptotics of the first moments of the number of high leaves, with and without a ballot constraint on the paths, for Brownian motion indexed by a regular tree, a continuous model in which the ballot computation is exact; they explain heuristically the logarithmic correction to the maximum of a branching random walk (a theorem of Bramson, Addario Berry and Reed, and Aïdékon), and so the constant three quarters in the Fyodorov Hiary Keating conjecture. New in this book.
- [Selberg central limit theorem for Delta as a statement about the characteristic function of the Pólya variable](../RESULTS.md#selberg_clt_for_delta_via_polya_characteristic_function) (Proposition 12.16, page 193; conditional on the Riemann hypothesis for L(s, Delta), for the Gaussian limit only): Expresses the log modulus of the L function of the discriminant form on its critical line through the characteristic function of its Pólya variable plus an explicit archimedean term, so that, under the Riemann hypothesis for this form, Selberg's central limit theorem becomes a statement about lognormal fluctuations of the characteristic function of an explicit probability law. New in this book.
- [The log modulus of the L function of Delta as the logarithmic potential of its Thorin measure](../RESULTS.md#log_modulus_of_delta_as_thorin_potential) (Proposition 12.17, page 194; conditional on the Riemann hypothesis for L(s, Delta)): Under the Riemann hypothesis for the discriminant form, the log modulus of the characteristic function of its Pólya variable on the real line is the logarithmic potential of the Thorin measure carried by the squared ordinates of the zeros, and it is strictly concave between consecutive zeros. New in this book.
- [The L function of Delta on the critical line through the extreme value variable M*](../RESULTS.md#critical_line_of_delta_through_extreme_value_variable) (Proposition 12.18, page 194; proved): Writes the L function of the discriminant form on its critical line, times a gamma factor, as the Fourier transform of a finite measure, the law of the logarithm of a gamma variable restricted to the event that it exceeds a scaled maximum of independent exponential variables, and identifies that measure with the central value L(6, Delta) times the law of the shifted Pólya variable. New in this book.
