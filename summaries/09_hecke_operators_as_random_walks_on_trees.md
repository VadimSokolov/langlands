# Chapter 9. Hecke Operators as Random Walks on Trees

Why do return probabilities of random walk on a free group (Kesten), eigenvalues of large random regular graphs (McKay) and eigenvalues of the Hecke operator T_q on modular forms of growing level or weight (Serre's vertical Sato Tate theorem) share one law, the Kesten McKay law? The chapter works on the Bruhat Tits tree of PGL(2) over the q adic numbers, the (q+1) regular tree on which T_q acts by adjacency. Its tree theorem writes the normalised nonbacktracking sphere operators as combinations of two Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind in the normalised adjacency operator and derives from this identity the Kesten McKay density and moments, the return probability and Green function of simple random walk, and convergence to the Sato Tate semicircle as q grows. The law is Macdonald's p adic Plancherel measure. In weight two and prime level congruent to 1 mod 12 the vertical theorem reads as Benjamini Schramm local convergence of supersingular isogeny graphs to the tree, the elliptic terms of the Eichler Selberg trace formula counting short cycles, with an exact finite level correction tested at 207 prime levels. Horizontal and vertical limits are compared, with Shin's Plancherel density theorem.

## From the literature

- Return probabilities on free groups and amenability (Kesten 1959); the eigenvalue law of random regular graphs (McKay 1981).
- The Bruhat Tits tree (Ihara 1966; Serre, Trees, 1980); spherical functions and the Plancherel formula (Macdonald 1971); the Ramanujan bound (Deligne 1974; Deligne and Serre 1974).
- The vertical Sato Tate theorem (Serre 1997; Conrey, Duke and Farmer 1997), its Maass form analogue (Sarnak 1987) and effective versions (Murty and Sinha 2009); the automorphic Plancherel density theorem (Shin 2012; Shin and Templier 2016).
- Local convergence of graphs (Benjamini and Schramm 2001); the sphere operator recursion (Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak 1988); the Sato Tate theorem (Barnet Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor 2011).

## New results

The individual statements of the tree theorem are classical; the book claims its formulation and single proof.

- [Tree theorem, a single Chebyshev proof of the Kesten McKay law and the Green function of the regular tree](../RESULTS.md#tree_theorem_kesten_mckay_chebyshev_proof) (Theorem 9.1, page 134; proved): One Chebyshev polynomial identity for the sphere operators of the regular tree, on which for prime q the Hecke operator acts by adjacency, yields the Kesten McKay spectral law, its moments, the return probability of simple random walk and its expected number of visits to the starting vertex, and convergence to the Sato Tate semicircle as the degree grows; the individual facts are classical, and the formulation and the single proof are the book's.
