# Chapter 6. Random Matrices and Families of L Functions

How do eigenvalues of Haar random matrices from compact classical groups model zeros of L functions, and how does that limit differ from the Sato Tate limit? The chapter states the Weyl integration formula (circular unitary ensemble, eigenvalue repulsion), proves through Gaussian vectors that Haar measure on SU(2) gives the semicircle law, and presents the Diaconis and Shahshahani theorem that traces of powers of a Haar unitary matrix are asymptotically independent complex Gaussians. It states Montgomery's pair correlation theorem for zeros of the Riemann zeta function under the Riemann hypothesis, the Katz and Sarnak symmetry types (unitary, symplectic, even and odd orthogonal) for one level densities of low lying zeros, and the Iwaniec, Luo and Sarnak theorem for holomorphic newforms, under the generalized Riemann hypothesis and for test functions of restricted support, with its consequences for nonvanishing at the central point, and conditional bounds for the average analytic rank of elliptic curves. It closes with the Keating and Snaith moment conjectures for the zeta function and quadratic Dirichlet L functions, and separates two random matrix limits: a fixed group at varying primes (Sato Tate), with vertical versions at a fixed prime (Birch, Kesten McKay), against groups of growing dimension near the central point. The chapter is expository and proves no new theorem.

## From the literature

- The Weyl integration formula (Weyl 1925 and 1926).
- Traces of powers (Diaconis and Shahshahani 1994; Diaconis and Evans 2001).
- Pair correlation (Montgomery 1973; Odlyzko 1987; Rudnick and Sarnak 1996).
- Symmetry types (Katz and Sarnak 1999; Deligne 1980).
- Low lying zeros of newforms (Iwaniec, Luo and Sarnak 2000); average analytic rank (Brumer 1992; Heath Brown 2004; Young 2006); rank data (Bektemirov, Mazur, Stein and Watkins 2007).
- Moments (Keating and Snaith 2000; Conrey and Ghosh 1998; Conrey and Gonek 2001; Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein and Snaith 2005; Jutila 1981; Soundararajan 2000; Shen 2021) and the ratios conjecture (Conrey, Farmer and Zirnbauer 2008).
- Vertical Sato Tate (Birch 1968; Serre 1997; Conrey, Duke and Farmer 1997).
