# Chapter 28. Haar Measure and the p* Formula

From *On Langlands Conjectures: Probabilistic Methods* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 28. The chapter sets Haar measure as the law of Frobenius on compact Sato Tate groups beside Haar measure as a prior in statistical inference. It computes left and right Haar measures and the modular function of the affine group of the line, which acts simply transitively on the upper half plane, shows that hyperbolic area is the image of left Haar measure, and presents Duke's equidistribution of Heegner points (CM points) as a noncompact analogue of Sato Tate. For transformation models it proves the classical theorems that Barndorff Nielsen's p* formula is exact and that invariant credible sets of the right Haar prior (Fraser's structural and Fisher's fiducial distributions) have exact frequentist coverage; in its examples the left Haar (Jeffreys) prior undercovers, with exact coverages for normal errors. The main new result concerns a conjugacy class, such as a Satake parameter, of a compact connected simple Lie group observed through noise whose density is a class function: Jeffreys' prior is Haar measure, whose image on classes is the Sato Tate measure, while the Berger Bernardo reference prior is uniform on the maximal torus, the arcsine law for SU(2). Haar normalisations of volumes, Tamagawa numbers and period formulas close the chapter.

## From the literature

- Existence and uniqueness of Haar measure (Haar 1933; von Neumann 1934, 1936; Weil 1940).
- Equidistribution of Heegner points (Duke 1988, after Iwaniec 1987 and Linnik 1968) and Waldspurger's formulas (Waldspurger 1981, 1985).
- Fisher's conditional and fiducial arguments (Fisher 1934, 1935) and structural inference (Fraser 1968).
- The p* formula and its exactness in transformation models (Barndorff Nielsen 1980, 1983).
- Right Haar priors and best invariant procedures (Stein 1965; Hora and Buehler 1966); marginalisation paradoxes (Dawid, Stone and Zidek 1973).
- Reference priors (Bernardo 1979; Berger and Bernardo 1992), for orbits (Chang and Eaves 1990).
- Volumes of spaces of lattices (Siegel 1945) and the Tamagawa number of SL2 (Weil 1961).
