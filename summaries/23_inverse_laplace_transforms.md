# Chapter 23. Inverse Laplace Transforms

How far can inverse Laplace transforms carry an L function between its Galois side, an Euler product of Frobenius traces, and its automorphic side, a modular form with zeros? The chapter reads an L function through four faces: the Lévy measure of log L, its sine squared transform (a Thorin density when the coefficients are nonnegative), the Pólya kernel (a modular form on the imaginary axis), and Thorin densities near the critical line. It collects the classical inversions and shows that Gaussian averages of the transported density recover every Frobenius trace with explicit error, that the Thorin measure of a self dual L function at a shifted basepoint is a superposition of folded Cauchy laws at the ordinates of the zeros, that atomic generalized gamma convolutions are Gamma times B spline variables, and that the Sato Tate law minimises the Good and Gaskins roughness among densities vanishing on the alcove walls. The example is the weight one eta product η(z)η(23z). Passing from Galois to automorphic data assumes Artin's conjecture (Hecke's theorem in the example); no new case of reciprocity or of the generalized Riemann hypothesis is proved.

## From the literature

Lerch's theorem and the Bromwich and Post Widder inversion formulas (Widder 1941), Post Widder as a law of large numbers (Feller 1971); Stieltjes inversion (Stieltjes 1894; Akhiezer 1965); Bohr's inversion and uniqueness theorems (Besicovitch 1932); Hecke's Mellin integral (Hecke 1936), Weil's converse theorem (Weil 1967) and weight one forms (Deligne and Serre 1974); the tail of the Thorin variable (Roynette and Yor 2005), here via Doetsch's transfer theorem (Doetsch 1974); Perron's formula (Perron 1908); B splines (Curry and Schoenberg 1966) and Pólya frequency functions (Schoenberg 1951); the Gamma times Dirichlet mean factorisation (James, Roynette and Yor 2008); roughness penalties (Good and Gaskins 1971, 1980; de Montricher, Tapia and Thompson 1975); minimal Fisher information (Huber 1974) and the alcove's Dirichlet spectrum (Bérard 1980).

## New results

New are the explicit Gaussian error bounds and the Good and Gaskins reading of the Sato Tate law; the Cauchy superposition restates for self dual L functions what Polson (2017) proved for xi.

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Explicit Gaussian window error for Bohr inversion of the sine squared transport](../RESULTS.md#gaussian_window_bohr_inversion_of_the_sine_squared_transport) (Theorem 23.5, page 424; proved): A quantitative form of Bohr's mean value inversion, in which Gaussian averages of the one minus cosine transform of a discrete signed measure recover the weight of each atom with an explicit, exponentially small error, so that the transform is injective. New in this book.
- [Frobenius traces recovered from the transported density with explicit error](../RESULTS.md#frobenius_traces_from_the_transported_density) (Corollary 23.6, page 425; proved): The von Mangoldt coefficients of an L function, and so the Frobenius traces of an Artin representation, are read off Gaussian averages of its sine squared transported density, with an explicit error bound at every finite window. New in this book.
- [Thorin measure at a shifted basepoint as zeros plus folded Cauchy noise](../RESULTS.md#zeros_plus_cauchy_noise_at_a_shifted_basepoint) (Theorem 23.9, page 429; proved): For a self dual L function the Thorin measure of the completed function, at a basepoint to the right of all zeros, is half a sum of folded Cauchy laws centred at the ordinates of the zeros, and moving the basepoint adds Cauchy noise, which restates for every self dual L function what Polson (2017) proved for the Riemann xi function. First stated in Polson (2017, version 26 of 2026), On Hilbert's 8th problem, arXiv:1708.02653.
- [Sato Tate law as the Good and Gaskins roughness minimiser, with shrinkage of symmetric power moments](../RESULTS.md#sato_tate_law_minimises_the_good_gaskins_roughness) (Theorem 23.12, page 436; proved): The Sato Tate law is the unique minimiser of the Good and Gaskins roughness penalty among densities on the alcove whose square root vanishes on the walls (an inequality of Huber for SU(2) and the Rayleigh principle in general), and for SU(2), near it, the penalised likelihood estimate shrinks each empirical symmetric power moment by an explicit factor. New in this book.
- [Stable recovery of Frobenius traces from a finite window of the transported density](../RESULTS.md#stable_recovery_of_frobenius_from_a_window) (Open Problem 23.1, page 438; open): Asks whether stable recovery of the coefficient at n of an L function from its transported density needs a window of length of order n, which would be an uncertainty principle for the Galois side. New in this book.
- [Good and Gaskins penalised likelihood for Thorin densities of L functions](../RESULTS.md#good_gaskins_penalised_likelihood_for_thorin_densities) (Open Problem 23.2, page 438; open): Asks for a roughness penalised likelihood estimate of the Thorin density of an L function from finitely many zeros or from a noisy transported density, the form of its maximiser, and a comparison of its error with exact inversion by Gaussian means. New in this book.
