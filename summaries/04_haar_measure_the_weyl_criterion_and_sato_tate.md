Why does the Sato Tate law for elliptic curves follow from automorphy, and which part of the argument is probability? The chapter proves the Weyl equidistribution criterion on compact groups from the Peter Weyl theorem, computes Haar measure on SU(2) (semicircle law, Chebyshev polynomials as characters of the symmetric powers, Catalan moments), and gives Serre's proof, through the Wiener Ikehara Tauberian theorem, that the normalised Frobenius traces of a holomorphic newform of weight at least two with trivial character are Sato Tate distributed when every symmetric power L function is holomorphic and nonvanishing on Re s = 1. For newforms without complex multiplication, potential automorphy and symmetric power functoriality supply that hypothesis. For a CM elliptic curve the limit law is an atom at zero plus an arcsine law, the image of Haar measure on the normaliser of the torus, and its Weyl fingerprint (the limits of the Chebyshev averages) is one exactly at multiples of four. Joint Sato Tate for two curves over Q without complex multiplication that are not isogenous over the algebraic closure makes their traces agree only on a set of primes of density zero. The chapter proves no case of functoriality: automorphy is the input, equidistribution the output.

## From the literature

- Weyl's criterion (Weyl 1916); the Peter Weyl theorem (Peter and Weyl 1927).
- The Sato Tate conjecture (Sato and Tate, in Tate 1965) and its reduction to symmetric power L functions (Serre 1968); the Wiener Ikehara theorem (Ikehara 1931; Wiener 1932).
- Sato Tate for elliptic curves over Q and newforms without complex multiplication (Clozel, Harris and Taylor 2008; Taylor 2008; Harris, Shepherd Barron and Taylor 2010; Barnet Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor 2011); symmetric power functoriality (Newton and Thorne 2021).
- CM curves and Hecke characters (Deuring 1953; Hecke 1918).
- Joint Sato Tate for curves that are not isogenous over the algebraic closure (Harris 2009).
- Vertical Sato Tate over finite fields (Birch 1968).

## New results

The theorems are due to others; the chapter contributes the exposition, complete proofs of the probabilistic side and numerical checks over the primes below ten million.
