What can probability theory say about the Langlands programme? The chapter states the reciprocity and functoriality conjectures through Satake parameters and Frobenius conjugacy classes and isolates their probabilistic shadow: normalised Frobenius classes should be equidistributed for Haar measure on a compact Sato Tate group. Frobenius traces of two elliptic curves at the primes below ten million illustrate the semicircle law of the Sato Tate theorem and the law of a curve with complex multiplication, which has an atom at zero. The chapter's own results express a limitation: equidistribution does not determine automorphy. Altering an automorphic sequence of Hecke eigenvalues on an infinite set of primes of density zero leaves every limiting average unchanged, while Ramakrishnan's refinement of strong multiplicity one for GL(2) makes the altered sequence nonautomorphic. So each automorphic sequence has continuum many nonautomorphic impostors with the same limiting averages, and no function of the limiting empirical laws of the traces characterises automorphy. Probability therefore enters the programme through consequences of the conjectures, as calibration and as a source of reformulations, not as a method of proving reciprocity, and nothing in the chapter proves a case of reciprocity or functoriality. A roadmap matches the methods of the book to its five parts.

## From the literature

Langlands' letter to Weil (Langlands 1967) and lectures (Langlands 1970); Artin reciprocity (Artin 1927); Weil's converse theorem (Weil 1967); modularity of elliptic curves over the rationals (Wiles 1995; Taylor and Wiles 1995; Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor 2001); the local Langlands correspondence for GL(n) (Harris and Taylor 2001; Henniart 2000); symmetric power functoriality for holomorphic newforms (Newton and Thorne 2021); Deligne's bound (Deligne 1974); the Sato Tate theorem (Clozel, Harris and Taylor 2008; Taylor 2008; Harris, Shepherd Barron and Taylor 2010; Barnet Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor 2011) through Serre's argument (Serre 1968); Hecke's equidistribution of the angles of a curve with complex multiplication (Hecke 1918); strong multiplicity one (Jacquet and Shalika 1981) and its refinement for GL(2) (Ramakrishnan 1994); converse theorems for GL(n) (Cogdell and Piatetski Shapiro 1994).

## New results

Both results are the authors' own and are proved in full.

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Equidistribution does not determine automorphy](../RESULTS.md#equidistribution_does_not_determine_automorphy) (Proposition 1.5, page 7; proved): Sato Tate equidistribution of a sequence indexed by the primes cannot certify that it comes from a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL(2) with trivial central character, because altering such a sequence on an infinite set of primes of density zero leaves the limiting empirical averages of all continuous functions unchanged but, by Ramakrishnan's refinement of strong multiplicity one, makes it nonautomorphic, and continuum many equidistributed sequences are not automorphic. New in this book.
- [Statistics unchanged by density zero alterations cannot characterise automorphy](../RESULTS.md#density_zero_invariant_statistics_cannot_detect_automorphy) (Corollary 1.6, page 8; proved): No statistic of sequences indexed by the primes that is unchanged when a sequence is altered on a set of primes of density zero, for instance any function of the limit points of its empirical laws, can tell an automorphic sequence from continuum many nonautomorphic ones. New in this book.
